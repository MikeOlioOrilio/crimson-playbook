01
Counts & court

Cross midcourt in 10 seconds

#

Get the ball AND your feet across.

  • The backcourt count starts when your team gains control of a live ball in its backcourt. Passing between teammates does not restart it.
  • While dribbling across, the ball and both feet must gain frontcourt status. The center line belongs to the backcourt.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Ten-second violation → opponents get a throw-in.

Picture this in a game

A pass is still flying toward a teammate in the frontcourt when the count reaches 10. Throwing it before 10 is not enough: it must gain frontcourt location in time.

02
Counts & court

Backcourt: you cannot take it back

#

Once established in frontcourt, protect that progress.

  • With team control in frontcourt, a violation occurs when your team is the last to touch there and the first to touch after the ball returns to backcourt. Stepping on the center line while holding it also counts.
  • If the defense tips the ball back, you may recover it. A throw-in may go directly into backcourt; special airborne-catch exceptions also exist.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Backcourt violation → opponents get a throw-in.

Picture this in a game

You pass backward across midcourt to your teammate. The violation happens when your team first touches the ball in backcourt—not merely when the pass crosses the line.

03
Counts & court

Inbounding: 5 seconds to release

#

Find a teammate. Pass. Then step in.

  • The count begins when the ball is at the thrower’s disposal. Release the pass into the court before five seconds expire.
  • For a designated spot, keep at least one foot on or over the three-foot-wide spot. You can move backward; this is not a pivot-foot rule. After a made basket, you may move along the end line.
  • Stay outside until releasing. A defender must not reach through the boundary plane before the pass is released.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Five-second violation → opponents inbound at the original throw-in spot.

Picture this in a game

Your first option is covered. Look to the safety immediately. A timeout request must be recognized before the violation; it cannot erase it.

Practice it: Made-basket entry →

04
Counts & court

Closely guarded: know the 5-second count

#

See the pressure. Make a decision.

  • In frontcourt, a defender within six feet can trigger a five-second closely guarded count on the ballhandler. Holding and dribbling are separate counts under the standard NFHS rule.
  • Utah high school modification: the count applies while holding, but not while dribbling. Do not assume your Bantam event uses that modification.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Five-second closely guarded violation → opponents get a throw-in.

Picture this in a game

Under the standard rule, holding for three seconds then starting a legal dribble ends the holding count and starts a new dribbling count. Once you pick it up, you cannot start another dribble.

05
Counts & court

The paint: offensive 3 seconds

#

Cut, show a target, then clear the lane.

  • The count applies in your offensive lane while your team controls the ball in frontcourt and the game clock is running. Lane boundary lines are part of the lane.
  • Get BOTH feet out to clear it. A shot ends team control, so there is no three-second count during rebounding action. Allowances exist for an immediate scoring move; do not use them as permission to camp.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Offensive three seconds → opponents get a throw-in.

Picture this in a game

One foot outside and one foot on the lane line? You are still in the lane. Step completely out and re-space.

06
Defense & fouls

When may we press full court?

#

Listen for our coach’s press call.

  • NFHS permits full-court defense throughout a game. Youth event rules can limit pressure by division, time remaining, or score margin.
  • Utah Bantam’s published page prohibits backcourt pressure with a lead of 20 or more in all divisions. Its additional last-five-minutes restriction lists younger divisions, not sixth-grade boys.
  • That page says event rules vary. Confirm your actual event in All Ball: Events → Event Detail → All Game Rules.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Local event rules determine the warning or penalty. Do not assume a universal press penalty.

Picture this in a game

If your event uses the published 20-point restriction and we go ahead by 20, drop back out of backcourt pressure immediately.

Practice it: Full-court man →

07
Feet & ball

Traveling: control your pivot foot

#

To dribble: ball out BEFORE pivot up.

  • Traveling is illegal foot movement while holding the ball. It is not simply “more than two steps.” The timing of control and your landing establishes the pivot.
  • Catch standing on two feet: either may pivot. Moving catch with one foot already down, or landing one then the other: that first foot is the pivot. Some jump stops leave neither foot available to pivot.
  • To start a dribble, release before lifting the pivot. To pass or shoot, you may lift it, but release before it returns; if you jump off it, release before either foot lands.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Traveling → opponents get a throw-in. The official rotates the fists.

Picture this in a game

Catching, planting a pivot, then sliding that foot to get comfortable is a travel. So is jumping with the ball and landing while still holding it.

Practice it: Front pivot →

08
Feet & ball

Carrying / palming & double dribble

#

Bounce it. Do not scoop and carry it.

  • A dribble ends when the ball comes to rest in one or both hands. Carrying, also called palming, happens when you let it rest in your hand and then continue illegally.
  • Touching with both hands ends the dribble. After picking it up, pass or shoot; bouncing again is a double dribble.
  • A high bounce alone is not a violation. The important question is whether the ball rested in your hand.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Illegal dribble → opponents get a throw-in.

Picture this in a game

You scoop the ball underneath, pause with it supported, then bounce again to beat your defender. That is illegal, even if the move looks smooth.

09
Free throws & check-in

Free throws: who can move when?

#

Lane = release. Shooter and outside = rim.

  • In a marked lane space: enter the lane when the shooter RELEASES. Do not enter the shooter’s semicircle until the ball hits the rim or the free throw ends.
  • Shooter: stay within your semicircle and behind the foul line until the ball hits the rim or the free throw ends. Outside players must stay behind BOTH the extended foul line and three-point arc until then.
  • The shooter has 10 seconds to release. Rebound only a free throw that will remain live after a miss—not the first of two, or a shot followed by an awarded possession.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

An early offensive entry can cancel a make. An early defensive entry can earn a replacement if the shot misses; a make normally counts. Let the official administer the result.

Picture this in a game

The shot only hits the backboard. That is not the “go” signal for the shooter or outside players. Wait for the rim; a free throw that misses the rim is a violation.

10
Free throws & check-in

Checking in: report, wait, enter

#

The official waves you in—not the buzzer.

  • When coach sends you, report to the scorer’s table with your jersey number and be ready to play.
  • Wait outside the boundary. At a legal substitution opportunity, the table alerts the officials; enter when an official beckons you onto the court.
  • A raised hand can help show you are ready. It does not give you permission to enter. During timeouts or breaks, follow the scorer’s and official’s directions.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Entering without reporting or permission can result in a technical foul.

Picture this in a game

The horn sounds while you are at the table. Stay outside until the official waves you in, then tell your teammate you are replacing him and find your assignment.

11
Defense & fouls

Defend & screen without illegal contact

#

Win position with your feet.

  • To establish legal guarding position, face the opponent with both feet on the floor. After that, legal movement is allowed; “feet moving” does not automatically mean a blocking foul.
  • Contest within your vertical space. Do not lean into a shooter, hit an arm, or move under the landing area. Hand pressure means activity near the ball—not holding or pushing the player.
  • Screen with a stable position and arms close. Give an unseen stationary defender a normal step, and a moving defender enough time and distance to avoid you.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Illegal contact can be a personal foul. Hands straight up alone do not guarantee a legal play.

Picture this in a game

A screener steps sideways into a defender at contact. That is an illegal screen, even with hands tucked in.

12
Defense & fouls

Personal fouls & the team bonus

#

Stay available. Play disciplined defense.

  • Under NFHS rules, five charged personal/technical fouls disqualify a player for the rest of the game. The team replaces him.
  • The fifth team foul in a quarter puts the opponent in the two-shot bonus for ordinary defensive common fouls. Team totals reset each quarter; fourth-quarter totals carry into overtime.
  • Shooting fouls have their own free throws. Player-control and team-control fouls do not earn bonus free throws. There is no NFHS one-and-one.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Officials report the foul and direct the next play. Coach tracks your foul count.

Picture this in a game

An offensive moving screen is a team-control foul: the other team gets the ball, not bonus free throws. A Bantam event using halves may publish a different bonus system.

13
Feet & ball

Lines, loose balls & saves

#

The boundary line is out.

  • Touching the sideline or end line makes you out of bounds. If you touch the ball while out, it is out too. The opponent of the team that last touched it gets the throw-in.
  • An airborne player keeps the location of the last floor contact. To save a ball, jump from inbounds and release before landing out.
  • The ball can travel through the air beyond a boundary without being out until it touches something out of bounds. Never chase a save into spectators or equipment.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Out of bounds → opponents get a throw-in.

Picture this in a game

You step on the sideline while holding the ball. It is out—even if the ball itself is still over the court.

Rule referencesNFHS case examples · boundaries and throw-ins (Abilene chapter) ↗
14
Free throws & check-in

Held balls, whistles & the arrow

#

Strong hands. Stop when the whistle blows.

  • When opponents both hold the ball so neither can gain control without undue roughness, the official calls a held ball. Do not keep wrestling.
  • Most held balls are resolved with the possession arrow: that team gets a throw-in. The arrow changes when its alternating-possession throw-in ends.
  • A game and each overtime start with a jump ball under NFHS rules. Youth events may use a different start procedure.
THE CALL / NEXT PLAY

Held ball → possession-arrow throw-in; no personal foul just for a legal tie-up.

Picture this in a game

You and a defender secure the rebound together. After the whistle, release calmly and listen for the throw-in direction.

YOU MAKE THE CALL

Six quick checks

Say your answer before opening each card.

1You are dribbling across midcourt. One foot is still behind the line. Are you fully across?

No. A dribbler needs the ball and both feet to gain frontcourt status. Get across early.

2The defender knocks your frontcourt pass back across midcourt. Can you retrieve it?

Yes. The defense caused the return. This differs from your team sending it back untouched.

3You stand in a marked free-throw lane space. Do you move on release or rim?

Release. The shooter and outside players wait for rim contact or the free throw to end. Stay out of the shooter’s semicircle until then.

4You lift your pivot foot and then release to start a dribble. Legal?

No. To start a dribble, release first, then lift. Passing and shooting have different pivot-foot rules.

5The scorer’s horn sounds. Can you step onto the court?

Wait for the official to beckon you in. The horn and your raised hand are not permission.

6We lead by 20. Can we keep pressing?

Check the event rule. Under the published Utah Bantam restriction described here, no backcourt pressure is allowed with a lead of 20 or more. Follow coach’s instruction to drop back.

Use this guide with your coach

This is a player-friendly guide to common situations, not every exception in the rulebook. The official decides the call. Ask respectfully, hand over the ball when requested, and get ready for the next play.
