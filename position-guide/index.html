Team View/Positions & responsibilities
FIVE ROLES · ONE CONNECTED TEAM

Know your job.
Learn every role.

A player’s number tells us what job he has in a play. It does not decide how tall he must be, where he must stay, or what skills he is allowed to learn.

For our sixth-grade team

These are starting responsibilities for a fast, smaller lineup using five-out offense and helping man-to-man defense. Everyone handles, passes, shoots, screens, defends, and rebounds. We rotate roles in practice. The coach’s assignment and the diagram for each play take priority over the usual position job.

1 Point guard2 Shooting guard3 Small forward4 Power forward5 Center

Every player’s responsibilities

On offense

Run into useful space. Show a target, catch ready to play, and choose a shot, drive, or pass. After passing, complete your cut or screen and fill an open spot. Keep the paint clear for the next driver.

On defense

Guard your assigned player; offensive position numbers do not dictate matchups. Pressure the ball without opening a straight drive. When off the ball, see your player and the ball, move with the pass, and talk through screens.

On every shot

Defensively: locate your matchup, establish legal rebounding position, then attack the ball with two hands. Offensively: know whether you crash or provide transition safety. In the self-rebounding shooting drill, follow every shot and pursue the rebound.

In every workout

Practice both-hand control, both-side layups, balanced stops and pivots, accurate passes, closeouts, and free throws. Build shooting range only while maintaining balance and a repeatable release.

1
ORGANIZE · ADVANCE · CONNECT

Point guard

Get us into an advantage, then keep everyone involved.

Offensive responsibilities

  • Get available for the outlet and scan up the floor. Advance by pass when a teammate has a clean advantage.
  • Call the action, protect spacing, and change pace. Use a retreat dribble to escape pressure instead of forcing a pass.
  • In five-out, pass and cut or screen like everyone else. You do not have to keep the ball to lead.

Defensive responsibilities

  • When assigned the ball handler, contain first: stay balanced and make the dribbler change direction without reaching.
  • Call out screens early and follow our coverage. If your player passes, move into your new help position.
  • If assigned transition safety, protect the basket first and communicate matchups as teammates recover.

When we inbound

Often the first receiver or safety outlet. Change speed to get free, meet the pass, catch with balance, and look upcourt. If denied, clear space for the next option. If you are the inbounder, make the read rather than forcing the first option.

When they inbound

If guarding their organizer, stay connected through cuts and screens. Make a catch difficult without losing the backdoor route. Once the ball is in, contain the first drive and communicate.

Shots to become reliable at

Layups with either hand; balanced catch-and-shoot shots in your current range; a short pull-up after one or two dribbles when the rim is closed; free throws. A floater is a later addition after footwork and basic finishes are dependable.

Skills to prioritize

Eyes-up ball control, weak-hand dribbling, retreat-and-reattack, passing on time, changing pace, and reading two defenders in a 2-on-1. Practice making the next pass before a trap closes.

Your cue: “See the floor. Make the next teammate better.”
2
SPACE · GET READY · ATTACK

Shooting guard

Be ready to score or create as soon as the ball arrives.

Offensive responsibilities

  • Maintain useful wing or corner spacing. Be ready before the catch, with hands available and feet organized.
  • Read a closeout: shoot when open and balanced, drive the available gap, or move the ball.
  • Cut behind overplays and use screens shoulder-to-shoulder. Be a second ball handler when the 1 is pressured.

Defensive responsibilities

  • Against a perimeter scorer, close out under control and contest without flying past the catch.
  • Stay alert to cuts and off-ball screens. Communicate before contact rather than following silently.
  • On the weak side, move toward help while keeping your own player in view. Recover on the pass; finish by finding a rebounder.

When we inbound

Often a receiver coming off a screen or a cutter to the basket. Wait for the screen to be set, read the defender, and show a target. If you are a decoy, run a convincing cut and clear space for the next option.

When they inbound

If guarding a shooting threat, know where the screen may come from. Call it, follow the team’s coverage, and arrive balanced on the catch. Do not watch the inbounder while your player slips behind you.

Shots to become reliable at

Catch-and-shoot shots from both wings and corners at a distance you can shoot well; layups with either hand after attacking a closeout; a controlled one-dribble pull-up; free throws. Three-pointers are an option when range and form support them.

Skills to prioritize

Shot-ready footwork, clean catches, quick pass/drive/shoot decisions, cuts behind denial, reading a screen, and finishing after one or two purposeful dribbles. Keep developing the handle to bring the ball up.

Your cue: “Be ready before the catch.”
3
CUT · CONNECT · PURSUE

Small forward

Connect the guards and screeners with movement and effort.

Offensive responsibilities

  • Run a wide lane in transition and cut hard when the basket opens.
  • Keep the weak side active: backcut an overplay, fill an empty spot, and be available for a reversal pass.
  • Attack a closeout with a purpose. On a teammate’s drive, adjust into a passing window instead of crowding the ball.

Defensive responsibilities

  • Be ready for different matchups. Use your feet and positioning against quick players and establish rebounding position against bigger players.
  • Recognize when you are the weak-side helper. Stop penetration when it is your help responsibility, then recover or communicate an exchange.
  • Track cutters and pursue long rebounds. Do not leak out before your team controls the ball.

When we inbound

Often the basket cutter, secondary receiver, or a screen-and-slip option. Time your move with the passer’s readiness. If the first cut is covered, clear to the assigned spot so the next cut has room.

When they inbound

If guarding the cutter, protect the path to the rim while seeing the ball. Call screens and avoid getting caught watching a decoy. After the catch, recover to the communicated matchup.

Shots to become reliable at

Layups off cuts on either side; catch-and-shoot wing and corner shots within your range; balanced finishes after a closeout attack; controlled putbacks and free throws.

Skills to prioritize

Changing speed on cuts, catching while moving, weak-hand finishes, passing after penetration, closeout-to-contain footwork, and locating an opponent before pursuing a rebound.

Your cue: “Find the space. Make the effort play.”
4
SCREEN · CONNECT · REBOUND

Power forward

Create room for teammates and turn effort into extra possessions.

Offensive responsibilities

  • In five-out, occupy perimeter space and make the same pass/cut/read decisions as a guard. Do not park in the lane.
  • Set a stationary screen with useful angle and timing. After the cutter clears, roll, slip, or space according to the play and defender.
  • Catch in the middle or short roll and look for a finish or a simple pass. Rebound offensively when assigned.

Defensive responsibilities

  • Talk early when your player screens. Use the team’s chosen coverage; do not invent a switch by yourself.
  • If you are the low weak-side helper, be ready to stop a drive and communicate the recovery. That job depends on location, not your number.
  • Find and box out your matchup, then pursue the rebound. If matched outside, contain and close out like a perimeter defender.

When we inbound

Often the first screener, a screener who becomes a receiver, or the inbounder. Get set before the cutter uses you. After screening, show a second target rather than standing still. The inbound passer needs composure and vision, not a particular height.

When they inbound

If guarding a screener, call the screen and stay responsible for that player’s slip or pop. Communicate any exchange so two defenders do not follow the same cutter. Find your matchup when a shot goes up.

Shots to become reliable at

Two-hand catches into balanced close finishes; either-side layups off a roll or cut; short bank shots and putbacks; catch-and-shoot elbow or short-corner shots within range; free throws.

Skills to prioritize

Screen angle and patience, reverse pivots, catching under pressure, short-roll passing, rebounding with two hands, and a clean outlet. Keep practicing ball handling and perimeter shooting rather than becoming only a screener.

Your cue: “Create space, then become an option.”
5
ANCHOR · FINISH · OUTLET

Center

Give us a dependable target and help finish defensive possessions.

Offensive responsibilities

  • Run toward the rim when transition spacing allows. If the pass is not there, move into the offense’s assigned space.
  • In five-out, the 5 also passes, cuts, handles, and screens from outside. Use a brief seal or inside catch when the play creates it, then clear the lane.
  • Catch with two hands, protect the ball, and finish with balance or pass out when help arrives. Avoid rushing a difficult shot into a crowd.

Defensive responsibilities

  • When assigned an inside player, establish position early and communicate cutters and screens in front of you.
  • Help protect the basket when your position and coverage call for it. Contest with balance; chasing a block must not replace guarding and rebounding.
  • Secure the rebound and find the outlet. If your matchup moves outside, move with the responsibility; the 5 is not a permanent zone defender under the rim.

When we inbound

Often a screen-and-seal target or the inbound passer. If screening, get set and then show your hands when the lane opens. If inbounding, scan the scoring option and safety early, pass to space away from pressure, then enter the court to become an outlet.

When they inbound

If guarding their inside target, protect the rim-side cut without losing the ball. If assigned the inbounder, obstruct passing windows legally and then follow them onto the court. Never assume guarding the inbounder means your job ends with the pass.

Shots to become reliable at

Close finishes with either hand; balanced bank shots; layups from rolls and cuts; putbacks after securing the ball; free throws. Add a short jump hook or simple pivot finish as the basic footwork develops, while continuing perimeter form shooting.

Skills to prioritize

Strong catches, balanced pivots, seeing the next pass, legal screening, rebound pursuit, and accurate outlets. Practice guarding in space and handling the ball so height today does not limit your future role.

Your cue: “Be a target. Finish the possession.”

Ball side and weak side: the same rules for all five

If your player has the ball

Contain the drive, contest with control, and communicate. Pressure should make the offense uncomfortable without giving away an easy lane.

If your player is one pass away

Be close enough to recover on the pass. Position in the gap or deny as the coach calls; do not automatically abandon an easy passing target to chase the ball.

If your player is on the weak side

Shift toward the middle enough to help while keeping your player and the ball in view. The weak-side low defender may have first help at the rim. Move back toward your matchup as the pass travels.

When the ball changes sides

Everybody adjusts. Ball-side and weak-side jobs change with the ball. Help briefly, communicate who has whom, and complete the stop with a rebound.

Inbound jobs are assigned by the play

A play needs a composed passer, a scoring option, useful screeners or decoy cuts, and a safety outlet. Any position can fill those jobs. Know your first action, your counter if denied, and where you go after the pass. On defense, name your matchup, call screens, protect the basket, and stay alert to the inbounder entering the play.

Match the timing and legal screening habits in the playbook. Be aware of the referee’s count and your designated inbound spot; use the coach’s safety option before the pass becomes desperate.

A simple practice check

Build the common skills first

Start with both-side finishing, shooting form, passing, and ball control. Give everyone repetitions as a handler, cutter, screener, and defender.

Add one role emphasis

Choose a priority from your role above, then test it with a defender in 1-on-1, 2-on-2, or 3-on-3. Rotate the jobs. Track good decisions and execution as well as made shots.

Before playing, answer: Where do I create space? Who do I guard? What is my inbound job? On a shot, do I rebound or provide safety?

Coaching basis & how to use this guide

These responsibilities are a coaching adaptation for this team, not fixed requirements attached to a player’s size. The conventional names come from Jr. NBA’s position overview. The emphasis on broad skill development and learning multiple roles follows USA Basketball’s foundational player-development guidance. Ball-side and weak-side teaching is informed by Breakthrough Basketball’s man-to-man defense examples. The specific role cues, shot priorities, and inbound jobs here are recommendations for this playbook, not quotations or a universal system.
