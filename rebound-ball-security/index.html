Securing the Rebound: Attack, Chin & Protect

A rebound is not complete until the ball is protected from guards digging down. This slow-motion studio isolation removes the court and crowd noise so players can focus strictly on hand placement, chin position, elbow width, and footwork.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The 5 Actions to Secure Every Rebound

Most turnovers after a defensive rebound happen in the first 1.5 seconds because the rebounder catches with one hand, brings the ball down to the waist, or turns blindly into traffic. Follow these five steps on every board:

Step 1 · Before Contact

Track Early & Show a Two-Hand "W" Target

As soon as the shot goes up and you establish body position, lock your eyes on the flight of the ball and prepare both hands above your shoulders.

  • Two hands every time: One-handed rebounds get tipped away or knocked loose on the way down.
  • Spread fingers wide ("W" shape): Thumbs point inward behind the ball so it cannot slip through your hands.
  • Jump from a balanced base: Take off from two feet so you can absorb contact in the air.
Step 2 · Peak Extension

Attack & Snatch at the Highest Point

Do not wait on the floor for the ball to come to you. Jump to meet the ball at the highest point you can control with two hands.

  • Aggressive squeeze: Clamp both sides of the basketball firmly with your fingertips and palms.
  • Arms extended, elbows slightly flexed: Absorb the momentum of the rebound and immediately initiate the downward pull.
Step 3 · The Descent

Pull Straight to the Chin & Land Wide

While coming down, rip the ball straight from overhead into your upper chest/chin pocket and land on a wide, stable base.

  • Land two feet wide (shoulder-width or wider): A narrow landing makes you easy to bump off balance into a traveling violation.
  • Hips low, knees bent: Land in an athletic stance ready to pivot immediately.
  • Never drop the ball to your waist: Keep the ball above your chest line away from smaller guards slapping at your hips.
Step 4 · The Protection Cage

"Chin It" with Elbows Flared Out

Tuck the basketball 4–6 inches directly beneath your chin with your elbows flared out wide at roughly 90 degrees.

  • Ball under the chin: Your head, shoulders, and forearms form a natural roof over the ball.
  • Elbows wide and strong (not swinging): Wide, stationary elbows establish your legal cylinder so defenders cannot reach in without fouling.
  • Locked wrists: Grip the ball tightly enough that a slap from underneath cannot pop it loose.
Step 5 · Transition

Rip-Through Pivot & Chin Up to Outlet

Once balanced, pivot away from pressure to put your body between the defender and the basketball while scanning the sideline for your guard.

  • Violent rip-through: If a defender crowds your front, sweep the ball forcefully either low-to-high (below the knees) or overhead to the opposite pocket.
  • Pivot to the outside sideline: Open toward the nearest wing/sideline rather than passing across the middle of your own basket.
  • Pass before dribbling: Look for the crisp two-hand overhead or chest outlet pass first; only dribble if the outlet is denied.
Coaching Cue in Practice: "SNATCH — CHIN — ELBOWS!"

During 3-on-3 and 5-on-5 drills, award a bonus point whenever a rebounder lands on two feet with the ball tucked under the chin and elbows wide before making the outlet pass.

Habit to Eliminate: The "Waist Drop & Immediate Dribble"

Sixth-grade players naturally bring the rebound down to their stomach or put the ball on the floor immediately in traffic—where guards sit waiting to strip it. Keep the ball high and protected until the outlet window is clear.
